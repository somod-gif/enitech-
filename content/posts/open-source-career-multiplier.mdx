Every engineer I know who landed a great role has a story that starts with
"it started with a pull request". Open source is the closest thing our
industry has to a public track record that compounds. Here's the case.

## A portfolio that verifies itself

Private projects are claims. Public repositories are evidence. When a
recruiter, a mentor, or the hiring manager looks at your GitHub, they can
read the actual code, see how you handle review, and watch how you structure
a commit history. No cover letter can do that.

Public code answers "can they actually ship?" faster than any resume bullet.

## Feedback from the world is free

The first time my code got reviewed by a stranger on the internet, I learned
three things about my own work in one comment. The cycle is brutal in the
best way: post a tool, watch a human interact with it, fix what's broken. You
can't buy a review loop that efficient.

## It teaches the habits of teams

Issues, PR templates, semantic linting, documented decisions, changelogs —
open source forces you into real-world engineering workflows before any
company time logs you in. You build the muscle of writing code that other
people read, and of reading other people's code.

## Work in public = opportunities find you

I've been invited to hackathons, mentoring circles, and a community
ambassadorship — purely because someone saw how I write issues, review
requests, or answer questions. When your work is visible, opportunity
doesn't need your application to find you; it just walks in.

## Start small, start this week

You don't need to design a new framework. You need to:

1. fix a documentation typo in a dependency you use,
2. pick a small issue and submit your first PR,
3. document the little tools you build to make your own life easier.

Each of those is a brick in a visible body of work — and the body of work is
the resume that eventually writes your recommendations.
