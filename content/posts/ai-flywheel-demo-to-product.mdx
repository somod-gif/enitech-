The pattern is always the same: a beautiful AI demo that professionals applaud, three months of quiet, and then a corpse of a roadmap. AI products decompose when they never get fed real usage data.

Here's the framework I build AI products to survive with.

## The flywheel: usage → data → quality

An AI product must be *better for the tenth user than it was for the second*. That only happens when the pipeline collects:

1. **Prompt and context** for every real request,
2. **The output** that was actually used,
3. **The feedback** — thumbs, edits, re-asks, time spent,

…and turns that feedback into improvement. The engineering constraint is plumbing, not intelligence: by the time you're gathering signals from production, the product is already better.

## Start with humans in the loop

Before you talk to "zero-shot autonomy", you build **human-review workflows**: the AI drafts, a human approves, corrections flows back into the training set. This costs nothing, keeps quality high where it matters, and — most importantly — starts the data flywheel in week one, instead of hoping "the data will come later".

## Enumerate what "good" looks like

You cannot improve what you cannot measure. For a checkout-copilot or support-autosuggester, define metrics early:

- acceptance rate of suggested completions,
- edit distance between draft and final,
- escalation rate out of AI resolution,
- time-to-resolution per session.

Each one gives you permission to optimize without wondering.

## The quality budget

The biggest engineering expense in AI products is **hygiene**: filtering bad inputs, deterministic guardrails on the money-path outputs, structured outputs parsing with retries, and a failure mode that degrades to "human asks a form" instead of dead-silent broken.

```ts
const result = await model.generate(prompt, { structured: aiResponseSchema });
if (!result.valid) return fallbackManualWorkflow(); // never let AI own the irreversible step
```

## Keep the loop closed in production

Ship the annotation loop before you ship the polish. Copy buttons, thumbs up/down, "why did this happen" — the features that look boring are the ones making the model honest.

The pattern in one line: **AI products are data products wearing a chat interface.** Build the data engine first, and the demo will turn into a product.
